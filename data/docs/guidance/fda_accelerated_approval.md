# FDA Regulatory Guidance: Accelerated Approval Program (21 CFR Part 314 Subpart H / Part 601 Subpart E)

## 1. Statutory Authority and Purpose
The FDA Accelerated Approval Program allows for earlier approval of drugs that treat serious or life-threatening conditions and fill an unmet medical need. Instituted in 1992 under 21 CFR 314 Subpart H (drugs) and 21 CFR 601 Subpart E (biologics), and formally codified under Section 506(c) of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act).

## 2. Surrogate and Intermediate Clinical Endpoints
- **Surrogate Endpoint Definition:** A laboratory measurement, radiographic image, physical sign, or other measure that is not itself a direct measure of clinical benefit, but is known or reasonably likely to predict clinical benefit.
- **Intermediate Clinical Endpoint Definition:** A clinical endpoint that can be measured earlier than irreversible morbidity or mortality (IMM) that is reasonably likely to predict an effect on IMM or other clinical benefit.
- **Standard of Proof:** Unlike Traditional Approval (which requires direct demonstration of clinical benefit such as improved overall survival or functional outcome), Accelerated Approval relies on surrogate endpoints reasonably likely to predict clinical benefit.

## 3. Post-Marketing Confirmatory Trials (Phase 4)
- **Mandatory Requirement:** Sponsors are required by law to conduct post-approval confirmatory trials to verify and describe the predicted clinical benefit.
- **FDORA Reforms (2022):** Under the Food and Drug Omnibus Reform Act of 2022 (FDORA), FDA may require that confirmatory studies be underway prior to granting accelerated approval.
- **Expedited Withdrawal Authority:** Under Section 506(c)(3) and 21 CFR 314.530 / 601.43, if a confirmatory trial fails to verify clinical benefit, or if the sponsor fails to conduct trials with due diligence, the FDA Commissioner has expedited authority to withdraw the indication or approval from the market (as occurred with Bevacizumab in metastatic breast cancer).
