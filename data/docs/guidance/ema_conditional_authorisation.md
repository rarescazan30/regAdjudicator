# EMA Regulatory Guidance: Conditional Marketing Authorisation (Regulation EC No 507/2006)

## 1. Scope and Legal Basis
In the European Union, Conditional Marketing Authorisation (CMA) under Regulation (EC) No 507/2006 allows for the early approval of medicines that address unmet medical needs for seriously debilitating or life-threatening diseases, emergency threats to public health, or designated orphan medicinal products.

## 2. Four Mandatory Criteria for Conditional Authorisation
A conditional marketing authorisation may be granted before comprehensive clinical data are available only if all four legal criteria are fulfilled:
1. **Positive Benefit-Risk:** The benefit-risk balance of the medicinal product is positive.
2. **Post-Authorisation Data Likely:** It is likely that the applicant will be able to provide comprehensive clinical data post-authorisation.
3. **Unmet Medical Need Addressed:** Unmet medical needs will be fulfilled.
4. **Public Health Benefit Outweighs Risk:** The benefit to public health of the immediate availability on the market outweighs the risk inherent in the fact that additional data are still required.

## 3. Key Differences: EMA CMA vs. US FDA Accelerated Approval
- **Positive Benefit-Risk Required Upfront:** Unlike FDA Accelerated Approval (which can be granted on a surrogate endpoint with plausible biological rationale alone), the EMA CHMP requires preliminary evidence that actual clinical benefit outweighs risk. EMA does not approve products on unvalidated biomarker surrogates without evidence of clinical relevance (which explains why EMA refused Aduhelm despite plaque reduction).
- **Annual Validity & Specific Obligations:** A CMA is valid for one year only, subject to annual renewal by the European Commission. The marketing authorisation holder must complete specific post-authorisation obligations within defined timeframes. Once comprehensive clinical data are confirmed, the CMA is converted into a standard 5-year / unlimited marketing authorisation.
