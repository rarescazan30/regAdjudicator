# Aducanumab (Aduhelm) - EMA Regulatory Summary

## 1. Regulatory Status & Approval Pathway
- **Drug Name:** Aduhelm (aducanumab)
- **Jurisdiction:** European Union (European Medicines Agency - EMA / Committee for Medicinal Products for Human Use - CHMP)
- **Application Procedure:** Centralised Marketing Authorisation Application (EMEA/H/C/005558)
- **Initial Regulatory Decision:** Refusal of Marketing Authorisation recommended by CHMP on December 16, 2021.
- **Re-examination & Withdrawal:** Following the initial refusal, the applicant requested a formal re-examination. However, on April 20, 2022, the applicant formally withdrew the application (EMA/CHMP/557556/2022) before the re-examination was finalised because the CHMP indicated that the grounds for refusal could not be overcome.
- **Current Status in the EU:** NOT AUTHORIZED / Refused. Aduhelm cannot be marketed or prescribed in European Union member states.

## 2. Therapeutic Indications & Patient Population
- **Proposed Indication:** Treatment of adult patients with mild cognitive impairment due to Alzheimer's disease and mild Alzheimer's disease dementia (early Alzheimer's disease).
- **Authorized Population:** NONE. Marketing authorisation was denied, and no subgroup of patients was approved for clinical use in the EU.

## 3. Clinical Trial Evidence & Endpoints
- **Evaluation of Pivotal Studies:** CHMP evaluated the two main Phase 3 double-blind, randomised clinical trials: EMERGE (Study 302) and ENGAGE (Study 301).
- **Key Scientific Grounds for Refusal:**
  1. **Conflicting Clinical Efficacy:** The two pivotal studies yielded contradictory results. EMERGE showed a small reduction in CDR-SB decline at 10 mg/kg, whereas ENGAGE showed no clinical effect or difference compared to placebo.
  2. **Unvalidated Surrogate Endpoint:** Although Aduhelm demonstrably reduced amyloid-beta plaques in brain PET imaging, the EMA/CHMP concluded that a causal relationship between plaque reduction and clinical cognitive/functional improvement had not been convincingly established.
  3. **Lack of Demonstrated Clinical Relevance:** The modest numerical differences in cognitive scales observed in post-hoc subsets did not establish a robust or statistically convincing clinical benefit across the overall target population.

## 4. Safety Warnings & Risk Management
- **Clinical Safety Concerns (ARIA):** CHMP identified frequent and significant safety risks associated with Aduhelm, specifically Amyloid Related Imaging Abnormalities:
  - High frequency of ARIA-E (cerebral swelling/edema) and ARIA-H (intracranial microhemorrhages).
  - A substantial proportion of patients required treatment interruption, dose reduction, or hospitalisation due to ARIA events.
- **Negative Benefit-Risk Balance:** In its final assessment (Section 3.7.2 of the Assessment Report), the CHMP concluded that the potential harms (brain swelling and microbleeds) clearly outweighed the uncertain, inconsistent, and unproven clinical benefits. Consequently, the benefit-risk balance was judged negative.
