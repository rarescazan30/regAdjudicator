# Bevacizumab (Avastin) - EMA Regulatory Summary

## 1. Regulatory Status & Approval Pathway
- **Drug Name:** Avastin (bevacizumab)
- **Jurisdiction:** European Union (European Medicines Agency - EMA / European Commission)
- **Initial Approval Date in Breast Cancer:** March 2007 (authorised under Centralised Procedure in combination with paclitaxel for metastatic breast cancer).
- **Post-Confirmatory Review (2010–2011):** Following the release of the AVADO and RIBBON-1 data, the CHMP initiated a formal Article 20 safety and efficacy review of Avastin in metastatic breast cancer.
- **Current Status in the EU:** APPROVED and MAINTAINED for first-line metastatic breast cancer in combination with paclitaxel.

## 2. Therapeutic Indications & Patient Population
- **SmPC Section 4.1 Approved Indication:** Avastin in combination with paclitaxel is indicated for first-line treatment of adult patients with metastatic breast cancer.
- **SmPC Section 4.1 Specific Note on Combination Regimens:** EMA removed the combination of bevacizumab with docetaxel from the European SmPC due to an unfavourable benefit-risk balance, but explicitly retained the combination with paclitaxel as a standard first-line option.

## 3. Clinical Trial Evidence & Endpoints
- **Progression-Free Survival (PFS) in the EU Regulatory Paradigm:** 
  - The CHMP acknowledged that confirmatory studies (AVADO, RIBBON-1) did not establish an Overall Survival (OS) benefit.
  - However, in the European oncology framework, the CHMP determined that a clinically relevant and statistically significant prolongation in PFS of approximately 5.5 months (achieved with paclitaxel in trial E2100) remained meaningful in delaying disease progression and maintaining patient quality of life.
- **Key Divergence vs. US FDA:** 
  - **US FDA:** Demanded demonstrated overall survival benefit or substantial quality-of-life improvements to sustain accelerated approval, revoking the indication in 2011.
  - **EMA / CHMP:** Concluded that PFS improvement in first-line metastatic breast cancer in combination with paclitaxel provides sufficient clinical value, and thus maintained the European marketing authorisation.

## 4. Safety Warnings & Risk Management
- **Identified Toxicities:** SmPC highlights hypertension, gastrointestinal perforation, arterial thromboembolism, proteinuria, and wound healing complications.
- **European Risk Minimisation:** Monitored via routine pharmacovigilance and detailed warnings in SmPC Section 4.4, with blood pressure monitoring recommended during therapy.
