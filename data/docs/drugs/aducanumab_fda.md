# Aducanumab (Aduhelm) - FDA Regulatory Summary

## 1. Regulatory Status & Approval Pathway
- **Drug Name:** ADUHELM (aducanumab-avwa) injection, for intravenous use
- **Application Type:** Biologics License Application (BLA 761178)
- **Approval Date:** June 7, 2021
- **Regulatory Pathway:** Accelerated Approval under 21 CFR Part 601, Subpart E
- **Statutory Basis for Approval:** Approved based on reduction in amyloid-beta plaques observed in the brains of treated patients. The FDA determined that reduction in amyloid-beta plaque represents a surrogate endpoint reasonably likely to predict clinical benefit (slowing of cognitive and functional decline).
- **Post-Marketing Requirement (Phase 4):** As a statutory condition of Accelerated Approval, the FDA required the sponsor (Biogen) to conduct a randomized, controlled confirmatory clinical trial (ENVISION/Study 303) to verify clinical benefit. (Note: Biogen subsequently discontinued commercial distribution of Aduhelm in 2024 to reallocate resources to Lecanemab).

## 2. Therapeutic Indications & Patient Population
- **Approved Indication:** ADUHELM is indicated for the treatment of Alzheimer's disease.
- **Target Population:** Treatment should be initiated in patients with mild cognitive impairment (MCI) due to Alzheimer's disease or mild dementia stage of Alzheimer's disease (early Alzheimer's disease), the specific population enrolled in the pivotal clinical trials.
- **Diagnostic Requirement:** Confirmation of the presence of amyloid-beta pathology prior to initiating treatment is recommended.
- **Population Restrictions:** The US label does NOT restrict prescription based on ApoE ε4 genotype status, though genetic testing is recommended to inform risk discussions.

## 3. Clinical Trial Evidence & Endpoints
- **Pivotal Clinical Studies:** Evaluated in two double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled Phase 3 studies in patients with early Alzheimer's disease:
  - **EMERGE (Study 302 / NCT02484547):** Met its primary endpoint at the high dose (10 mg/kg), demonstrating a statistically significant 22% reduction in clinical decline on the Clinical Dementia Rating-Sum of Boxes (CDR-SB) compared to placebo (difference: -0.39, p = 0.012).
  - **ENGAGE (Study 301 / NCT02477800):** Failed to meet its primary endpoint on CDR-SB (difference: 0.03, p = 0.83), producing conflicting clinical data.
- **Surrogate Biomarker Evidence:** In both trials, Aduhelm demonstrated a statistically significant, dose-dependent, and time-dependent reduction in brain amyloid-beta plaque burden measured by Amyloid Positron Emission Tomography (PET) composite standard uptake value ratio (SUVR). The FDA relied on this surrogate endpoint to grant Accelerated Approval despite the discordant clinical outcomes between EMERGE and ENGAGE.

## 4. Safety Warnings & Risk Management
- **Boxed Warning for ARIA:** Monoclonal antibodies directed against aggregated beta-amyloid, including ADUHELM, can cause Amyloid Related Imaging Abnormalities (ARIA):
  - **ARIA-E:** Brain edema or sulcal effusions (observed in 35% of patients in the 10 mg/kg group vs. 3% on placebo).
  - **ARIA-H:** Brain microhemorrhages and superficial siderosis (observed in 20% of patients on 10 mg/kg vs. 7% on placebo).
- **ApoE ε4 Genetic Risk:** Incidence of ARIA was significantly higher in ApoE ε4 homozygotes (43%) compared to heterozygotes and non-carriers (20%).
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Mandatory brain MRI monitoring is required at baseline, prior to the 7th infusion (first 10 mg/kg dose), and prior to the 12th infusion.
